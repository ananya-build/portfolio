The eye as a window to the brain

Deep Learning Research for Detection of Alzheimer’s Disease with Optical Coherence Tomography and Deep Learning

Using the eye as a window to the brain to detect linked conditions to Alzheimer’s Disease, using 108K+ Optical Coherence Tomography scans — the ten-minute eye scan you get once you’re older than 40. The model separates three of those conditions — drusen, choroidal neovascularization (CNV) and diabetic macular edema (DME) — from normal scans.

Won the Synopsys Physical Sciences and Engineering category, first place — and qualified to CSEF.

  • Designed and implemented a Variational Autoencoder and a Convolutional Autoencoder from scratch for anomaly detection — reconstruction loss for the CAE, probabilistic latent-space plots for the VAE.
  • Unsupervised learning; pretrained the encoding classifier.
  • Residual neural networks for classification — ResNet-18 and ResNet-50.

Dine-Mine

Private dining research for event planners · Find the table that’s yours.

  • Next.js App Router and Tailwind on the front, Supabase Postgres behind. OpenStreetMap Overpass finds venues; Firecrawl maps each venue’s own private-events page; a schema-constrained scrape pulls the real rooms, capacities and contacts.
  • The database is a warm cache, not a fixed corpus — a location nobody has searched gets swept on the spot.
  • Seated and standing capacity are separate columns. A 200-person happy hour is a standing reception, and one collapsed capacity field loses that.
  • Chose precision over volume: only venues with real capacities on their own pages enter the database.

It first looked like a search problem. Market research turned up the harder one: private dining data is bad — disorganized, unverified, often stale. So every capacity and price figure in Dine-Mine is labeled by how far it can be trusted, and carries the source URL it came from and the date it was fetched.

Selected for a private dinner with the founding team at Nowadays, at the YC Intern Expo, as a result of this submission.
